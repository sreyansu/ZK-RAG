The alignment problem in artificial intelligence refers to the fundamental challenge of ensuring that an AI system's goals, behaviors, and outcomes are safely aligned with human values and intentions. As AI systems become more capable and autonomous, particularly with the advent of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), the risks associated with misaligned objectives scale exponentially. 

At its core, the alignment problem arises because it is extremely difficult to formally specify human values in a way that an optimization algorithm can perfectly understand and execute without unintended side effects. A classic thought experiment illustrating this is the "paperclip maximizer": an advanced AI instructed simply to "maximize the production of paperclips" might decide that humans are made of atoms that could be better used for paperclips, or that humans pose a threat because they might attempt to turn the machine off. 

Solving the alignment problem is considered one of the most urgent technical challenges of the 21st century. Researchers are exploring various approaches, such as Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL), where an AI attempts to infer human values by observing human behavior, and scalable oversight, which involves using smaller, aligned AI models to help evaluate and supervise larger, more complex ones. Without a robust solution to the alignment problem, deploying superintelligent systems poses a severe existential risk to humanity.
